How to use it

  1. Portfolio and risk. Enter your account value and pick what you're willing to lose on one trade. 0.25% of $100,000 is $250. Start at the low end — the percentage is the single biggest driver of how long you survive a losing streak.
  2. Entry price. The price you actually filled at, not the level you planned. A fill that slipped changes every number below it.
  3. Low of day. The session low at the moment you enter. This anchors the whole structure: it is where the final stop sits and what defines 1R.
  4. Stop cushion. Placing the last stop exactly at the low invites a wick to take you out before the move. A cushion of 0.5% below the low is the common choice — it widens 1R slightly, which shrinks your share count, so it is a real trade-off rather than free insurance.
  5. ATR %. Optional, from your charting platform. It powers the two sanity checks in the chips: whether your entry is too far off the low, and whether 2R is realistically reachable.
  6. Read off the ladder. Buy the share count shown. Place all three stops immediately. Set alerts at +2R and +4R. Then follow the stages below.

What the two checks mean

LoD distance — how far above the session low you entered, measured in ATR. Under 60% is a clean entry. 60–70% is acceptable only when volume already confirms the breakout. Above 70% you are chasing: the stop is far away, so the same price move earns you far less.

2R in ATR units — how many average ranges the stock must travel to reach the first profit stage. Around 1.5–2 is reachable inside the first three sessions. Above 3 and the +2R shave will rarely trigger before Day 3.

Five nuances

ADD Stopped out before Day 4

Set a price alert at the high of the day you were stopped. If it triggers, add back half of what remains — inverse pyramid.

10,000 shares in −6,666 stopped out (stops 1 and 2) ───── 3,334 remaining +1,667 added back (50% of remainder) ───── 5,001 → 50% net size, not 33%

Sized this way, a later stop-out at the low still keeps total risk under 1R.

ADD Sideways pause after Day 4

A tight consolidation while under 4× ATR above the 50-MA is an add, not a trim. Inverse pyramid 50% of your net size.

Adding small keeps your average price from moving much, so it stays below the 10-MA stop and the trail keeps working.

TRIM Catalyst gap trade

On a news or earnings gap, +2R can arrive within half an hour of the open. Take the 33% there anyway — the rule is the level, not the clock.

At the Day 1 open, shave another 33%.

STOP Above +4R before Day 4

Consolidate everything to a single breakeven stop straight away, ahead of the normal Day 3 schedule.

You accept giving back unrealized profit. But holding the original stops at a parabolic point means risking a trip back below your entry, which makes no sense from there.

STOP Gap down below the 10-MA

No special handling. Run the same opening-range routine as stage S4 — five minutes, then the stop goes to the range low.
